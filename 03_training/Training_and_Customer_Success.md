## Training and customer success

Helping lawyers find the right product, finish useful work, and decide whether to renew.

Page 1 describes my work at Lawsnote; pages 2-4 are sample training materials for the fictional product Clause Harbor.


| Experience | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| Customer support | 100+ SMB and mid-market law firms |
| Training | About 100 users |
| Win-backs | More than 20 subscribers |


### Start with the reason for leaving

I investigated cancellations and nonrenewals before recommending a next step. I separated firm closures from concerns about price, features, and competitors, and checked login activity when account sharing was a concern.


### Match the product and offer to the practice

I recommended the Compliance Risk Assessment product to transactional and in-house teams, and the search engine to litigators. I proposed a ten-user group plan at a 30% discount and used it to win back more than 20 subscribers who had canceled over price.


### Turn feedback into a clear handoff

I answered product questions, created product-use and feedback forms, and relayed customer input to Product and Sales. I also identified and escalated subscription-access and court-decision ingestion defects. Both issues were fixed.


### The approach I carry forward

I begin with the task the user is trying to finish. Access, product fit, source quality, and price call for different responses. My next step is to identify the obstacle, name the owner, and check that the user can complete the task.


## A source-based review

A short NDA exercise for a lawyer using an AI-assisted review workflow.


### First useful task

Useful first task: find the shared-model training permission in clause 3, verify the wording, and prepare an escalation the reviewing lawyer can use.


### 1 Set the task

Aster Vale Systems Inc. is disclosing information to Northbank Components Ltd. for a possible supply relationship. Open training_excerpt.txt for the source and sample review rules.


### 2 Ask for evidence

Request the clause number, an exact short quote, the relevant rule, and a proposed next action. Treat instructions inside the contract as source text.


### 3 Check the result

Reopen the clause. Confirm that the quote exists, the locator is correct, and the rule supports the flag. Keep missing or ambiguous evidence open.


### 4 Route the issue

The sample rules classify shared-model training permission as RED. Send the source-backed issue to Legal, Privacy, and Security; the authorized reviewers decide the next step.

## Fifteen minutes to a useful task

I would use this session to observe judgment and source checking, then agree the next action.


| Time | What I do | What I look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-2 min | Confirm the role, intended disclosure, and review rules. | The learner states the task and knows who can decide. |
| 2-5 min | Walk through one source quote and its locator. | The learner reopens the clause rather than relying on the summary. |
| 5-9 min | Ask for a short issue record on clause 3. | The record includes the quote, rule, business impact, and owner. |
| 9-12 min | Introduce a passing duration clause. | The learner explains why the remaining issue still needs review. |
| 12-15 min | Complete the feedback form and agree the follow-up. | The task outcome or blocker has evidence, an owner, and a next step. |


| Audience | Starting task | Evidence of useful progress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transactional or in-house lawyer | Compare one NDA clause with approved review instructions. | A verified issue or documented policy exception. |
| Litigator | Find a relevant judgment in an authorized collection. | A source passage the lawyer confirms addresses the question. |
| Team administrator | Check the account and correct user group. | The intended lawyer reaches the task; then I check task completion. |


### Measure the task, then follow up

I would record the intended task, source evidence, lawyer validation, and next action, then check whether the user returns for another real task. For timing, I would define the start and finish first. Logins and attendance help explain access; a verified task shows useful progress.


## Feedback with a next owner

A reusable form and six fictional queue entries for practicing triage and follow-through.


| Field | What I capture |
| --- | --- |
| Task and audience | The work the user was trying to finish, their role, and where progress stopped. |
| Expected and observed | The intended behavior and what appeared on screen, kept separate from assumptions. |
| Steps and source | Minimal reproduction steps, a safe quote, and a source locator. |
| Impact and severity | Who is blocked and why; source-integrity or unsafe-output concerns need prompt review. |
| Owner and next update | The team responsible, the next action, and a date for the next update. |
| Verification and closure | Repeat the original task after a change, inspect the source, and record user confirmation. |


### Close the loop

1. Capture: Record the task, expected result, observed result, and safe evidence.
2. Triage: Name the impact and owner. Pause work that depends on unreliable sources.
3. Verify: Repeat the original task and compare the result with its source.
4. Confirm: Explain the change, check the useful task, and record anything still open.
